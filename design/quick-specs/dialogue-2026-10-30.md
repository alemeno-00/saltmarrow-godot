# Quick Design Spec: Talking (social encounters and dialogue)

**Type**: New Small System
**Status**: PROPOSAL — Julian's two answers are in (see Decisions); the rest is for him to approve or change.
**Scope**: Conversations with people Thomas meets below, and later with the townsfolk: choices, skill tests rolled
with the fight dice, and what they lead to. Not included: talking during a fight, a reputation system, voice acting.
**Date**: 2026-10-30
**Estimated Implementation**: about a week for the system and the first four encounters; more for each new one.

## Overview

Julian: "there should be some social / dialogue elements in the game, obviously with the townsfolk but it would be
good to have that as the player delves deeper." The deep is full of people with reasons to talk: survivors who want
out, the Faithful who want Thomas to come with them, the Drowned who half-remember, and Ellen, whose voice reaches
him through the star. Conversations play like Baldur's Gate 3: a portrait, what they say, a few numbered choices.
Some choices only appear for certain backstories, stats, or things Thomas has read in his journal. Risky ones are
tests, rolled with the same 2d10 power roll as a fight.

## Core Rules

1. **Talking happens before a fight, never during one** (Julian). Some people who notice Thomas speak first instead
   of attacking. Once anyone attacks, it is a fight until it ends.
2. **A conversation** shows the speaker's portrait and words and 2–5 numbered choices. Walking away is always one,
   unless the speaker won't let him.
3. **Choices are opened by who Thomas is**: his backstory (Fury, Shadow), his stats, what he has read (a lore page
   in the journal), injuries or star-changes he carries. A locked choice isn't shown.
4. **Tests.** A risky choice says which stat it uses (Might to intimidate, Agility to slip a hand, Reason to work
   something out, Intuition to read someone, Presence to persuade). It is a power roll, 2d10 + the stat:
   - **11 or less**: it goes wrong (they turn hostile, lie, or he gives something away);
   - **12–16**: it works, at a cost (oil, an item, a promise, a wound to his nerve);
   - **17+**: it works cleanly.
   Edges and banes apply as in fights (a lore page that helps is an edge).
5. **What a conversation can lead to**: walking past a band unseen or unchallenged, a fight that starts on Thomas's
   terms (surprise), a guide to the stairs, an item or oil, a lore page, an ally who follows for a level, a fight
   with more enemies than before, or nothing at all.
6. **Echoes across lives** (Julian). Now and then someone Thomas met in an earlier life says something they
   couldn't know: his name before he gives it, a word from that earlier conversation, his sister's song. They
   don't understand it, look confused, and carry on. It is flavour, never a mechanic: nothing is unlocked by it,
   and it is rare (about one echo per life at most), so it stays unsettling.
7. **Dialogue lives in data files** (one per speaker, under `scripts/data/dialogue/`), so writing new conversations
   needs no code.

## Variants: the star rewrites people (Julian, 2026-10-30)

Every encounter is a product of the star's reality-warping. Ellen's letters already reword themselves each life;
the people below do the same. **The same person can be someone different from one life to the next**: another
temperament, another past, another intent, as if they had lived a different life. A miner who was honest and
terrified in one life is calm and lying in the next, or kind where he was cruel. Nothing in the game explains this
outright; it is the star, and the player works it out.

1. **Encounter types, each with several variants.** A type is the shape of the encounter (a barricaded survivor, a
   preaching band, a trade, a plea that is really a trap). Variants change who it is, what they want, whether they
   mean it, and how it can end.
2. **Built from parts, chosen per life.** Each encounter is assembled from:
   - **who**: a person from a cast (survivors are Mara's shift: the same names as the tally tags, so a miner found
     dead in one life may be alive and pleading in the next);
   - **personality** in this life: frightened, bitter, gentle, cruel, serene, broken (the star's rewriting: the same
     person draws a different one in different lives);
   - **intent**: honest, lying, already Faithful, too far gone;
   - **want**: out, oil, a message carried to Mara, for Thomas to come with them;
   - **where**: a sealed room, a ledge over the sewage, a dead end.
   Which tests matter and what a conversation can lead to follow from the personality and intent.
3. **Seeded per life**, like the levels: a life always deals the same encounters, a new life deals new ones.
4. **Fresh across lives.** The game prefers people and combinations Thomas hasn't met yet (the journal's memory knows
   which he has). When someone does come round again, changed, that is where the echoes belong (rule 6): the same
   miner, a different man, and he knows Thomas's name without knowing why.
5. **Fixed beats vary within bounds.** Garrick is always at the bottom, but his temper and what he says shift with
   the life, the lore Thomas has read and how the level went; he never stands down. Ellen always reaches Thomas deep
   down, her words following the letters he has found.
6. **The first set**: about 4 types with 3–5 variants of each part. Claude drafts the lines in chat for Julian to
   review before any go in the game (as with the lore texts).

## The first encounters (below, in the Sewers)

1. **A survivor in a sealed room.** One of Mara's lost shift, half-changed, barricaded in. Wants out, offers oil
   and the way to the stairs. Reading him (Intuition) shows whether he is still himself or already Faithful and
   leading Thomas into a band. Which miner, and what this life made of him, varies.
2. **A Faithful band that stops to preach.** They would rather Thomas came willingly. He can play along (Presence)
   and walk through, ask about Ellen (an answer that changes with her letters he has read), or refuse, and they sing.
3. **Garrick, before the boss fight.** He knows Thomas has his tally tag's twin in Mara's ledger. With the right
   lore pages, Thomas can ask about his men and the First Shift; a strong test makes him hesitate (Thomas starts
   the fight with an edge, or a gate already shut). He never stands down.
4. **Ellen's voice.** In the deepest rooms she speaks to him through the star, in snatches. Answering her is a
   choice with no test: what he says shapes the next letter he finds.

**The townsfolk** (Agathe, Mara, Bram, Tobias, Pip, Nell, Rook, Corwin) need the town, which the brief has out of
scope. The smallest version: a stop at the surface between lives, one scene, two or three people to talk to before
each descent. That is a scope decision for Julian.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A Faithful band can be talked past, and failing the test starts the fight.
- [ ] A choice locked behind a lore page appears only once that page is in the journal.
- [ ] No conversation can start once a fight has begun.
- [ ] An echo happens at most once a life and changes nothing but the words.
- [ ] The same seed gives the same encounters; a new life gives different people, or the same people changed.
- [ ] Over five lives, no two encounters with the same person play the same way.
- [ ] Playtest: talking feels like a real alternative to fighting, not a detour.

## Decisions

1. (Julian, 2026-10-30) **No talking once a fight has started.**
2. (Julian, 2026-10-30) **People don't remember Thomas across lives**, but sometimes "just know" something as if
   they'd heard it before, don't understand it, and move on.
3. (Julian, 2026-10-30) **Encounters vary and are dealt at random each life**, and **the star rewrites people**:
   the same person can have a different personality, past or intent in another life.
4. *Open:* the surface stop between lives, so the townsfolk can be talked to (changes the brief's scope).
5. *Open:* where talking sits in the build order (after sneaking, which it shares "before a fight" with, is the
   natural place).
